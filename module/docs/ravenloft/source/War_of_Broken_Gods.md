# v0.3.0 — War of Broken Gods / Final Wish

Canon/design additions supplied by the GM:

- Vecna has already left Barovia and must not physically rejoin the battle, but his interest in the Unkindness is piqued. In dire circumstances he may whisper offers from another plane.
- Orcus and Vampyr must feel like powers that cannot be defeated merely by reducing a large pool of hit points. A Wish-level metaphysical intervention is required to make final victory possible, echoing older-edition encounters in which Orcus could not otherwise be truly overcome.
- There are two intended Wish routes.
  1. Damien's djinn has already expended its normal Wish, but wants to survive. When the party appears genuinely lost—or when Orcus/Vampyr defeats Strahd and threatens to inherit the ending—it can sacrifice its remaining Wish-granting connection to produce one final Wish. The djinn survives, is diminished/freed from this extraordinary obligation, and will not simply hand out another Wish casually.
  2. Vecna can provide the Borrowed Ending from beyond Barovia in exchange for an explicit secret/memory/mark/lore bargain. Accepting and using this route substantially increases Vecna Attention.
- The recommended Wish creates the Edict of Finality: until Barovia ends, released Dark Powers cannot anchor themselves there, inherit Strahd's prison, or reconstitute after destruction.
- Before the Edict, Orcus/Vampyr can be wounded and contained but reform after reaching 0 HP. Show this once to prove the rule, then avoid repetitive unwinnable grinding.
- If Orcus/Vampyr lands Strahd's final defeat before Wish, use a three-round inheritance clock. Without Wish, Strahd dies but the domain acquires a new supernatural anchor rather than being freed.
- Vecna may also make lesser emergency bargains to individual PCs. These are voluntary, explicitly priced, and affect future Arc Attention.


# VALLAKI PRELUDE — THE LOCKS ARE OPEN

Before Castle Ravenloft, Vallaki shows the first consequences of the Amber prisons breaking. Lady Wachter and her sons are dead, but her Vampyr cult remains. Without its leader it has become fragmented, ecstatic and increasingly convinced that the First Thirst will choose a successor more worthy than Strahd. The cult of Orcus fights it street by street, preaching death without return as the only honest escape from Barovia.

Van Richten has concluded that these are no longer ordinary cults. Their symbols answer divinations. Corpses react before necromantic rites are completed. Blood sigils develop magical resonance without a conventional caster. Something beyond the cultists is answering.

His warning is simple: the Unkindness broke locks that needed to be broken if Strahd was ever to die properly, but the locks did not distinguish Strahd from the powers imprisoned beside him. Strahd may no longer be the most dangerous being in Barovia.

Van Richten cannot prove the exact solution. He infers that a Dark Power requires an anchor, that the anchor must be broken, and that Strahd's final death may create an inheritance moment. The party should not allow Orcus or Vampyr to land or possess the decisive ending. If a manifestation is destroyed and simply reappears, then the problem is metaphysical permission rather than hit points. At that point Van Richten suspects only reality-rewriting magic such as Wish may be enough.

An optional Vallaki intervention lets the group weaken one cult, evacuate civilians, strike both at real resource cost, or preserve all strength for Ravenloft. The choice changes starting Orcus Pressure / Vampyr Claim but does not replace the castle finale.
