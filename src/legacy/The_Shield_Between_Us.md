# THE SHIELD BETWEEN US
## An eight-session Ironfist campaign

**Curse of Strahd I continuation | GM-only planning module | Version 1.0**

A dwarf returns from death in a body of metal. His homeland welcomes a hero, his brother welcomes a second chance, and his commanders welcome a weapon. Beneath the mountain, a real war is approaching. The question is not whether Thorgrinn can hold a shield wall. It is who he will allow to stand behind it.

**Format:** Exactly eight sessions, planned around four-hour meetings. Each session contains six keyed scenes, 215 minutes of playable material, a 15-minute break and 10 minutes for recap and closing feedback. Timings are pacing budgets, not promised player behaviour. Use the compression notes rather than adding a ninth session.

**Party assumption:** Thorgrinn, Damian, Vesper, Legna and Bunch, starting approximately level 10 after Ravenloft. Preserve their actual sheets, homebrew abilities and equipment. Milestones: level 11 after Session 2, level 12 after Session 5, level 13 after Session 8. Offset progression if their actual starting level differs; reassess encounter numbers rather than treating the offset as automatic balance.

**Campaign promise:** A sincere homecoming, unresolved brotherly love, class-based dwarven politics, an orc-and-goblin war with genuine antagonists and negotiable factions, and a conclusion determined by the party's alliances and choices.

**Status of this document:** This is a complete scene-by-scene arc plan with playable homebrew encounter profiles, evidence, handouts and contingencies. The encounters are not playtested or assigned official challenge ratings. Existing campaign rulings take precedence over the suggested procedures.

# 1. Continuity and the adventure's central truth

## Established foundation

Thorgrinn is still mechanical and soul-coin powered. He has not returned to his original dwarven form. This is the user's explicit correction and overrides the recap that generalized restoration to the entire party. Do not invent an inability to eat, drink, feel touch, sleep or experience pleasure; use whatever JP's actual character sheet and prior play establish.

The Ironfist Society is a stratocracy governed by high-ranking generals, not a monarchy. Rank governs class, resources and political power. The clan sells protection, enforces harsh martial law, maintains engineered roads and builds fortified outposts called Fingers. Thorgrinn and Baldrik grew up impoverished, joined military training together and served as a dual patrol in Neverwinter. They split when Thorgrinn prevented Baldrik's ritual execution of desperate thieves. Baldrik subsequently joined the Righteous Vanguard. [S1]

The later campaign adds important developments: the brothers met again in Barovia; Baldrik defended the wealth and equipment his service had brought him but privately wanted reconciliation. He still carried their old shield with its two mismatched rivets. The Vanguard's willingness to kill its own suspected deserters was demonstrated. Baldrik subsequently saw Thorgrinn's corpse; Damian told him resurrection might still be possible. These scenes must not be reset to their original estrangement. [S2-S3]

Thorgrinn's sword compulsion was lifted at the Raven Queen's shrine. Do not reactivate it to manufacture this arc's drama. His new struggle is what to do with freedom, not another compulsory berserk episode. [S4]

## New material, clearly separated from canon

Everything named below beyond the existing party, Baldrik, the Ironfists, the Righteous Vanguard, Sergeant Dagnner and their established history is a new proposal for this continuation. The hold's name, its exact position, additional relatives, generals, Cinder-Tusk clan, engineering works, politics and timetable are not claims about earlier sessions. No parentage, parental death or previously unconfirmed backstory is silently established.

Use **Kharad-Varr**, the principal Ironfist hold, in a mountain within practical reach of the clan's Sword Coast contracts. Its precise position on an official map remains unfixed. This avoids forcing an unverified geographic choice into Thorgrinn's history.

Two new family members make the drama multigenerational without replacing unknown parents: **Aunt Heddra Ironfist**, a lower-tier household steward, and **Runa Ironfist**, Heddra's adult daughter and a military engineer. Neither is secretly evil. They disagree about what the brothers owe their home.

## The GM's truth: what is actually happening

Six months before the party returns, Marshal-General **Hadrik Varr** authorizes a new diversion at the **Ninth Sluice**, routing water and heat toward military foundries. He knows the works violate an old compact with the mixed orc-and-goblin **Cinder-Tusk clan**, which occupies the deeper mountain. He suppresses the engineering warnings and describes the compact as obsolete.

The diversion poisons the clan's fungal beds and leaves several wells unusable. Cinder-Tusk raiding parties seize pumps and provisions. Some raids are retaliatory; others become predatory. Their war-chief, **Korga Emberjaw**, chooses escalation and conquest, including attacks on dwarven noncombatants. The clan's grievances do not excuse those choices. The war is real, not a staged invasion, and Korga is not Hadrik's hired accomplice.

Baldrik returns from his Barovian escort work, by an arranged Vistani crossing, before the party's escape. This return is a new connective event, compatible with his recorded intention to leave. Hadrik assigns him to inspect the diversion. Baldrik sees civilian suffering but signs a misleading report calling the disputed lower districts an enemy support zone. He tells himself his authority will let him protect people later. He does not know the full purge scheme at the beginning.

Hadrik now plans **Last Bastion**: reverse the mountain's pressure network and scour the Cinder-Tusk warrens with superheated water. He has concealed the likelihood that Lowhearth, the dwarven labourers' district, will flood too. The scheme serves military dominance and concentrated political power. It is not powered by captive souls and has no secret connection to Thorgrinn's creation.

Thorgrinn's return was not predicted. Hadrik opportunistically seeks his endorsement and voluntary technical cooperation. He cannot magically command Thorgrinn, switch off his body or extract his soul by invoking a military regulation. His claims about restoring Thorgrinn are political bait, not a new objective truth.

## The dramatic argument

Baldrik believes: "If I gain enough authority, nobody will ever make us helpless again."

Heddra believes: "A family cannot be saved by sacrificing everyone outside it."

Runa believes: "If people like us refuse every commission, only people like them will build the future."

Hadrik believes: "Protection requires obedience; those who refuse protection have chosen to be enemies."

Thorgrinn's answer belongs to JP. Do not prescribe forgiveness, leadership, restoration or revolutionary politics as the correct ending.

# 2. Running the eight-session structure

## The calendar

Session 1 covers the first two days: escape, a night at the Fifth Finger, then arrival and celebration. Session 2 is the following day; Session 3 the next; Session 4 the next. Session 5 occurs on the sixth day after arrival. Session 6 covers the following day and night. Session 7 begins the next morning; Session 8 follows the same afternoon.

This calendar is adjustable. Enemy plans advance when the party takes significant downtime, not because the real-world clock reaches a scheduled reveal. Give warnings before a delay costs a district or military position. Success can prevent an event; do not recreate it elsewhere merely to preserve a scene.

The party can long rest between Sessions 1-6 under the usual campaign rules. Before Session 7, provide an explicit opportunity to recover. Sessions 7-8 form one operational day. The pressure emergency at the end of Session 7 leaves approximately four hours before a major rupture: enough for a short rest and preparation, not a routine overnight rest. Explain that before resources become a trap.

## Only two campaign tracks

**Defence Readiness (D):** Starts at 2; minimum 0, maximum 6. Measures functioning pumps, organized defenders, intact roads and evacuation preparation. It never represents whether the party is "good."

**Civilian Accord (A):** Starts at 0; minimum 0, maximum 4. Measures practical cooperation among Lowhearth workers, Cinder-Tusk noncombatants and lawful military officers. It is not universal friendship or forgiveness.

Award or deduct only the changes explicitly stated in keyed scenes, once per achievement. Repeating a speech or paying repeatedly cannot farm a track. Equivalent creative solutions earn the same outcome. Any direct party attack on protected civilians reduces A by 1, once per incident; use ordinary consequences too, without turning this into a hidden alignment score.

At the war council in Session 7, disclose the practical effects. D 0-1: one unassigned defence front falls back, losing stores and territory, but civilian escape remains playable. D 2-3: unassigned fronts hold with damage. D 4-6: organized defenders hold them and provide one reinforcement benefit. A 0-1: no cross-clan assistance. A 2: civilians share safe routes. A 3-4: a Cinder-Tusk engineering team helps evacuate both populations and Korga risks losing authority if he refuses reasonable terms.

**Baldrik's development uses events, not a persuasion meter.** Record whether he (1) admits his signed report, (2) refuses an unlawful order in front of his soldiers, and (3) submits evidence and himself to judgement. All three create the possibility of accountable reconciliation. None forces Thorgrinn to forgive him. A private apology without action changes little.

## Fair procedures and failure

Use the table's normal 5e framework. Suggested checks are DC 13 for routine pressure, DC 16 for demanding work and DC 19 for exceptional feats. Select ability and proficiency from the player's approach. A suitable spell, sound plan or authoritative document can solve a problem without a roll.

A **work challenge** requires three progress before two setbacks unless the scene says otherwise. Each character contributes once before anyone repeats; repeated approaches need a genuinely changed method. A failed challenge still supplies the essential clue or route, but costs time, position, supplies or readiness. Do not roll to know information that an NPC has plainly volunteered.

A **timed encounter** uses actual combat rounds. Never run three-success challenges simultaneously without stating which actions advance which objective. Work on a mechanism normally costs an action. A relevant expenditure of a third-level-or-higher spell slot can count as one progress when its described effect genuinely helps; this is an optional homebrew adjudication, not a blanket spell rule.

**Political evidence is not an Insight contest.** People can know the truth yet disagree about policy. A failed persuasion check changes concessions, conditions or timing, not whether authenticated papers exist. Charm, truth magic, flight, teleportation, divination, invisibility and resurrection should work according to existing campaign rules. They shorten investigations or create new solutions; they are not arbitrarily disabled.

## High-powered party contingencies

Do not turn every dwarf guard into a boss because the party is powerful. Most soldiers cannot beat the Unkindness. Their leverage is their loyalties, knowledge, logistics and the lives they are responsible for. A general killed in public leaves a political problem, not an invincible replacement general.

If the red dragon ally survives Ravenloft and accompanies the party, honour the relationship. It can defend exterior roads, transport people or intimidate an army; the interior pressure galleries are too narrow for its full body. It is not automatically hostile to preserve difficulty. If magic allows it inside, reward the advantage and adapt space honestly.

If they bring shield guardians, mounts, a dragon egg or substantial treasure, assign actual effects. Guardians can hold a war front; the egg can remain with a trusted NPC chosen by the party, without a compulsory kidnapping. Funded food, compensation or repairs can earn an appropriate track increase once. Existing magic items are not confiscated just to make this arc work.

If Baldrik learns about Thorgrinn's survival in the final Barovia sessions, remove his surprise at the first reunion; retain relief, guilt and fear. If Ravenloft changes the party roster, reassign spotlight tasks to the actual characters rather than inventing their presence.

# 3. Cast, agendas and relationships

## The family

**Baldrik Ironfist - existing character; proposed current role: Vanguard captain.** A scar crosses his eyebrow; bronze clasps hold his iron-grey beard; the repaired shield remains with him. Publicly clipped and martial, privately more hesitant. He wants his brother alive, respected and close, but initially confuses protection with deciding for him. His material success and brutal conduct are both real. He knows he signed an unjust report; he does not initially know Last Bastion will destroy Lowhearth. His red line is deliberately handing Thorgrinn over for disassembly, but he may still wrongly believe a restrictive military commission is protection. Opening line: "I saw you dead. I had not decided what to say at your grave."

**Heddra Ironfist - new aunt; household steward, no military rank.** Keeps the lower-tier communal kitchen and family accounts. Warm, stubborn and capable of being unfair when frightened. Wants the family reunited without surrendering its conscience. She accepted Baldrik's money for repairs and food, and hates that this enabled her to avoid asking how it was earned. She knew he had reported Thorgrinn dead and protected that story because she believed it. She is not a secret revolutionary commander. Line: "I am glad you came home. That does not mean I am glad about everything we did while you were gone."

**Runa Ironfist - new adult cousin; junior military engineer.** Precise speech; soot-stained sleeves beneath a newly issued rank sash. Proud to have risen from labour status. Designed an efficient pump component without knowing Hadrik would bypass its safeguards; later noticed the misuse and hid her calculations out of fear of losing the family's advancement. Wants the mechanism repaired and her work recognized. She is ashamed, not an automatic betrayer. She will examine Thorgrinn only by invitation. Line: "I did not build a weapon. I built a valve. Then somebody decided the difference was paperwork."

## The Ironfist authorities

**Marshal-General Hadrik Varr - new principal political antagonist.** One of three council generals; commands offensive operations. Controlled, generous in public, genuinely convinced coercion preserves civilization. Wants emergency command over all three portfolios, the Cinder-Tusk expelled and Thorgrinn's reputation attached to the operation. Knows the civilian cost of Last Bastion. Will negotiate a lesser sentence or resign to retain allies if defeated politically; he is not obliged to fight to the death. Line: "You understand the cost of hesitation better than most. That is why your people need you beside us."

**General Brynja Flintward - new garrison commander.** Practical defender with little patience for aristocratic displays, but still shaped by the caste system. Wants a hold that survives and soldiers who obey lawful orders. Requires credible engineering evidence before stopping an active operation. Supports suspending the purge when shown the civilian risk, even if she dislikes the party. Line: "Show me the failure point. Then show me who will hold the gate while we change the plan."

**General Odran Brassmantle - new contracts and provisioning commander.** An accountant-soldier who measures promises in wagons and winter rations. Benefits from the system but is not Hadrik's secret co-conspirator. Wants external contracts maintained and a defensible settlement. Evidence of concealed liability, lost water, unlawful requisitions and a workable replacement supply plan moves him. A bribe without a plan does not. Line: "You may be right. Explain how everyone eats after you prove it."

**Sergeant Dagnner - existing scene name, spelling taken from the transcript.** The severe Vanguard sergeant who examined the party's damage and later considered their bounty. In this new arc she supervises escorts and requisitions. Loyal to a functioning company and its pay, not blindly to Baldrik. Has copies of cargo receipts and sealed orders. Can become a dangerous witness, an opportunistic intermediary or a combatant. A protected pension for her troops and evidence she will otherwise be scapegoated may buy cooperation. Her testimony requires corroboration. Line: "You call it greed. My soldiers call it getting home with wages."

**Sister Yrsa Coalbraid - new shrine keeper and veteran medic.** Offers spiritual counsel without demanding a preferred body. Recognizes continuity of personhood through relationships, testimony and the willing person's own account; her care does not depend on winning a magical identity trial. Can verify soldiers' injuries, preserve records and supervise an ethical future restoration inquiry. She cannot casually promise a return to flesh. Line: "A body can need work without its inhabitant needing permission to exist."

## The Cinder-Tusk clan

The Cinder-Tusk are one mixed orc-and-goblin clan with families, trades and internal factions. Neither ancestry predicts loyalty, kindness or competence. Military enemies include both peoples; protectors and negotiators include both too.

**Korga Emberjaw - new orc war-chief.** An effective tunnel commander who treats the diversion as proof that coexistence is impossible. Has expanded legitimate defence into conquest and knowingly ordered attacks that endangered civilians. Wants the upper reservoir, the foundry gates and political dominance. Respects enforceable power and concrete terms, not a single charming speech. Will stand down if his military position collapses and his people have a credible alternative, or accept defeat in a negotiated command challenge. Line: "When they took our water it was policy. When we take their gates it is savagery. Explain the difference without lying to me."

**Urzha Deepwell - new orc civilian speaker.** Former caravan negotiator, responsible for the displaced households. Opposes Hadrik and Korga's expansionism without pretending every raid was justified. Wants water, secure habitation, return of prisoners and accountability on both sides. Will not accept peace bought with a massacre of her own people. Line: "You can stop a war without telling me the dead were a misunderstanding."

**Skrik Coilhand - new goblin engineer.** Runs the clan's pumps and has organized sabotage as well as rescue work. Owns responsibility for disabling a lift that injured dwarven workers; will help repair it as part of a settlement. Wants mutual access to the regulators and binding limits on unilateral diversion. Needs Runa's upper-system calculations; Runa needs his lower-system maps. Line: "Your map ends where our homes begin. That is the first mistake."

**Minor reusable actors:** Edda, Fifth Finger warden; Venn Slate, records clerk; Tovik, adult mine foreman; Brakk, Cinder-Tusk tunnel captain; Ketta, goblin sapper who can be captured or recruited. Give each one job and one memorable habit. They are not additional conspiracy layers.

## Spotlight by player character

Thorgrinn determines his relationship with Baldrik, his bodily boundaries and what public responsibilities he accepts. Damian can expose the financial case, negotiate a workable compact and address the dissonance between how he described Thorgrinn's death and what he did to save him. Vesper can secure witnesses, advocate for living people affected by doctrine and guide consent-based spiritual inquiry. Legna can challenge militarized faith from experience while commanding a defence front. Bunch can win the trust of people overlooked by rank, coordinate evacuation and work with the competing engineers through ingenuity or magic. These are invitations, not prescribed attitudes or exclusive skill gates.

# 4. The mountain and its evidence

## Locations and travel

**The Fifth Finger:** A road fortress approximately three miles from the party's point of arrival. Nine miles of good military road connect it to Kharad-Varr's outer gate. Couriers can reach the hold before a party that sleeps at the outpost.

**Gate Crown and Hammerhall:** The upper entry, council chambers, military offices and great celebration hall. Broad bridges permit formal parades. Lower-tier civilians require sponsors for administrative access, not for basic emergency shelter.

**Lowhearth:** The damp labour district two major lifts below Hammerhall. Heddra's kitchen, family rooms, a shrine and communal workshops lie here. The party can walk between them in minutes. A service stair gives an independent route if the lifts fail.

**Ninth Sluice:** Half a day below Lowhearth by guarded transit tunnels. Three galleries control water and geothermal flow. Its side route reaches the Treaty Stair in an hour.

**Treaty Stair and Cinder Warrens:** The stair is the old neutral meeting point. Below it lie Cinder-Tusk dwellings, wells and fungal gardens. The path to the Vanguard Redoubt takes two hours; guided service passages can halve that.

**Vanguard Redoubt and Heartworks:** The redoubt guards the final military approach. The Heartworks, one hour beyond, contain the reservoir regulator, relief outlets and the Bastion maintenance engine. A narrow service passage bypasses the main approach if discovered or supplied by Skrik.

Use these connections as an encounter map, not a claim to official geography. Give the players a simple vertical sketch at arrival; add lower routes as they learn them. Travel times are for ordinary movement and yield to effective magic.

## Essential revelations: three access routes each

**R1 - The diversion caused the crisis.** Visible flow markers and dead fungal samples at the sluice; Runa's original diagrams; Urzha and Skrik's dated well records. No single destroyed ledger can erase all three.

**R2 - Baldrik knowingly misrepresented civilian districts.** His signed report in the military archive; the duplicate held by Dagnner; his own account when confronted with affected families. A signature proves he signed it, not why; let him explain without erasing the harm.

**R3 - Last Bastion will also destroy Lowhearth.** Runa's bypass calculations; Skrik's corresponding lower-outlet survey; the installed pressure plates and emergency manual in the Heartworks. All point to the same danger independently.

**R4 - Korga plans more than recovery of water.** Orders carried by a tunnel captain; military preparations witnessed in the warrens; Urzha's account of a rejected limited settlement. This prevents the adventure from reducing the clan to innocent scenery.

**R5 - The catastrophe can be prevented without killing either population.** The original operating manual; joint work by Runa and Skrik; direct inspection of the three regulator stations. Control requires coordinated action, not Thorgrinn's soul.

## Evidence packets used in play

E1: A sluice gauge record proving the diversion predates the first recorded raid. E2: A requisition showing foundry profits rising during the shortages. E3: Baldrik's signed classification of occupied districts as military support territory. E4: The old Water-and-Stone Compact, authenticated by matching dwarven and Cinder-Tusk copies. E5: Hadrik's sealed Last Bastion order. E6: The paired engineering assessment showing Lowhearth's predicted flooding. Copies are valid evidence when authenticated. Truth magic helps verify testimony but cannot establish facts an honest witness never knew.

## What will happen without intervention

Hadrik uses the welcome to solicit endorsement, a raid to justify emergency powers, the public register to bind the hero to a commission, and the invasion to activate Last Bastion. Korga moves raiders to the upper wells, intimidates dissenting clan leaders, then attacks the hold. Baldrik tries to keep his family safe by retaining rank and delaying moral decisions. The party can break any of these chains. There is no required assassination, kidnapping, capture or betrayal by a party member.

# 5. Session One - The Son Who Came Home

**Purpose:** Let the players enjoy surviving Barovia before introducing the costs of coming home. Establish affection, the social order and Baldrik's changed circumstances. No compulsory combat.

**Prepared actors:** Edda, Heddra, Runa, Baldrik, Hadrik, Brynja, Odran, Dagnner. **Props:** A military death register, feast seating, the old shield. **End question:** Will Thorgrinn allow his return to be used as an endorsement?

## 1.1 - Beyond the last mist | 20 minutes

**Place and actors:** A mountain road; the party only, plus any NPCs they actually rescued from Ravenloft.

Read: "The mist breaks. The cold has a clean edge to it. Pine trees move in a wind that does not whisper your names. On the road below, a work crew's hammers strike stone in a familiar rhythm. Above a distant watchtower hangs a white fist gripping an iron nail."

Thorgrinn recognizes the emblem automatically. Give each player one brief question: what proves this is different from Barovia, and what are they carrying forward? Do not describe the destruction of native Barovians beyond the actual finale's established outcome. The new arc does not retroactively decide who escaped.

Offer three approaches: walk to the Finger, send a scout or rest out of sight. None triggers an ambush. A passing road crew provides directions and water. The exact magical mechanism keeping the party together is proposed continuation fiction, not an additional curse. Preserve any different escape method established in the finale.

**Exit:** The Fifth Finger opens its outer gate. Thorgrinn sees the old rank markings he once knew.

## 1.2 - Recognition at the Fifth Finger | 35 minutes

**Actors:** Edda, two ordinary guards, a courier and a stranded supply crew.

Edda initially treats the party as travellers. When Thorgrinn identifies himself, she remembers his company and asks how he survived. This is an opportunity for shared history, not a test that can revoke his identity. Baldrik's old message lists him dead. Companions, recognisable habits and volunteered memories persuade her that the register needs correction. She offers shelter immediately.

A damaged wagon winch blocks the supply yard. The group can repair it, shift the load, use magic or organize the crew. This optional three-progress work challenge introduces practical heroism without requiring a fight. Success restores a shipment: D +1. Failure causes delay, not injury or a lost welcome.

A courier carries the story to Kharad-Varr while the party rests. Reports describe returning Ironfist kin and travellers claiming to have defeated a vampire lord; officials learn the details through witnesses and evidence, not instant omniscience.

**Exit:** The party chooses what proof and message to send. Heddra receives news before the generals finish composing theirs.

## 1.3 - The door that still opens | 40 minutes

**Actors:** Heddra, Runa, neighbours and the party; Baldrik has been summoned but has not arrived.

The next day, Heddra meets them in Lowhearth before the official reception. Her first words are "Come inside." She asks before embracing Thorgrinn. She offers the same hospitality to every companion and lets JP establish what food, drink and physical comfort mean in his present body.

Runa is fascinated by Thorgrinn's construction, notices herself treating him as an engineering mystery and apologizes. A direct boundary is respected. Heddra's repaired home shows Baldrik's money has genuinely improved lives. Beneath the repairs, damp walls, ration marks and military allocation stamps reveal persistent inequality.

Invite JP to establish one harmless childhood detail: a game, a household saying or a familiar hiding place. Do not ask for new traumatic canon. Heddra kept a memorial token because she believed Baldrik's report. She did not knowingly erase Thorgrinn.

**Clue:** Runa's work sash bears the Ninth Sluice assignment. She says shortages are causing trouble below, without giving the entire conspiracy away.

**Exit:** The feast summons arrives, addressed to all five heroes.

## 1.4 - A table above their station | 55 minutes

**Actors:** The three generals, Dagnner, Heddra, Runa, ordinary veterans and the party.

Make the celebration sincere. Veterans ask about tactics; craftspeople offer repairs; a singer gets an amusing detail wrong and invites correction. Each companion receives a personal toast based on deeds they choose to describe. Heddra is seated beside Thorgrinn, not humiliated at the door. Social tension appears through quiet astonishment that a low-ranked household occupies the high table.

Hadrik offers the party hospitality and Thorgrinn an honorary commission. He wants a public sentence supporting "the security of our mountain." Let characters ask what that means. The written commission can be inspected: it includes service obligations and restrictions on speaking for the clan. It is not a magical contract.

Brynja asks whether the heroes will inspect an attacked water installation. Odran asks how long they intend to remain. Dagnner recognizes them and carefully avoids discussing the Barovian bounty in front of the crowd.

**Choices:** Accept honour without commission, negotiate terms, accept knowingly or refuse. None cancels the feast. No poisoning or staged assault spoils the welcome.

**Exit:** Baldrik enters after the toasts. He is holding the old shield.

## 1.5 - Two mismatched rivets | 40 minutes

**Actors:** Baldrik, Thorgrinn; companions may stay if invited.

Move to a quieter side chamber. Baldrik's first reaction is relief rather than disgust. He asks, "May I?" before touching Thorgrinn's shoulder. The familiar shield is real; his affection is real; neither settles the past.

Baldrik says he saw the body, believed Damian's promise might be desperation and did not know how to live with their unfinished conversation. If Damian is present, he asks why Thorgrinn's death was described as an inconvenience. Let Damian explain his actions and tone; do not adjudicate who loved Thorgrinn correctly.

Baldrik wants another chance but offers it in the language he knows: lodging, rank, armour and protection. He does not offer a complete confession yet. A private conversation cannot solve years of conduct. If Thorgrinn refuses contact, Baldrik leaves the shield nearby without demanding forgiveness and agrees to communicate through someone Thorgrinn chooses.

**Exit:** Before leaving, Baldrik quietly asks the group to inspect the Ninth Sluice. He knows its official description is incomplete, but has not admitted that.

## 1.6 - The sealed dispatch | 25 minutes

**Actors:** Baldrik, Dagnner, a wounded adult courier; the party can witness or receive the dispatch secondhand.

A genuine raid has struck a lift beyond the Ninth Sluice. Workers and soldiers are trapped. The courier saw orc shield-bearers and goblin engineers cutting pump lines; no one has yet verified why that particular installation was targeted.

Dagnner delivers Hadrik's order to classify the route as a combat zone. Baldrik initially agrees with securing it, then hesitates at a clause permitting summary detention of "supporting households." He does not yet refuse.

The party can request command, volunteer independently or decline official service while helping workers. All lead to the same location with different access and authority. A refusal to help at all is allowed: the attack continues, and survivors bring evidence into Session 2 rather than forcing the party to travel.

**Close:** Heddra asks Baldrik, "When they say households, do they mean kitchens like ours?" End on his answer or inability to answer.

**Compression and record:** Shorten the parade, not the homecoming or reunion. Record the commission terms, Thorgrinn's boundaries, any D increase and what Baldrik now knows about the resurrection.

# 6. Session Two - The Price of Protection

**Purpose:** Show that the invasion is dangerous while exposing the difference between defending people and defending policy. The first major combat is an objective-based rescue, not an ancestry-based extermination.

**Prepared actors:** Baldrik, Brynja, Tovik, Brakk, Ketta and captured raiders. **Maps:** A lift approach and a 70-by-50-foot sluice gallery with three levels, service walkways and a control station. **Milestone:** Level 11 at the end.

## 2.1 - What the wounded actually saw | 25 minutes

**Actors:** Tovik, two injured workers, Yrsa and the party.

Yrsa is treating survivors. One worker saw raiders cutting the military feed line, not the household pipes. Another saw Brakk strike a surrendered guard. Both are true. Tovik says earlier complaints about dying lower wells were filed as enemy propaganda.

Basic care, introductions and listening reveal this information without rolls. Medicine, investigation or relevant magic can distinguish blast injuries from weapon wounds and establish which gallery failed first. Better information gives advantage on the first relevant navigation check, not an otherwise unavailable route.

A missing adult shift is still below. Put their names on a small roster so the rescue is about people rather than tokens. Civilians are not bait for an inevitable atrocity.

**Clue:** E1 begins with the dated gauge sheet Tovik carries: the new diversion started before the first raid in the log.

**Exit:** Brynja offers a transport cage and soldiers to hold the upper approach. The party decides whether Baldrik accompanies them.

## 2.2 - The order and its limits | 40 minutes

**Actors:** Brynja, Baldrik, Hadrik's written orders; Dagnner if she is escorting them.

The official task is to restore the pump, free the shift and identify attackers. A separate clause authorizes clearing all occupied chambers below the installation. Baldrik initially calls it ordinary operational language. Brynja agrees to narrow the party's orders if they point out the lack of identified targets.

This scene establishes command boundaries. Persuasion may improve troop support or access to records; the heroes do not need permission to avoid harming noncombatants. Accepting a commission does not grant magical obedience. If the party refuses the mission, Brynja still permits a civilian rescue under a neutral banner.

Dagnner wants recovered equipment counted. Damian can spot that requisitions exceed the garrison's actual needs. Legna can question whether a declared combat zone removes obligations to captives. Bunch can ask who will feed the trapped shift afterward.

**Exit:** Provide a written rescue mandate or note their independent status. It will matter when Hadrik later describes their actions.

## 2.3 - The broken descent | 35 minutes

**Actors:** Tovik as guide; trapped adult workers across a lift shaft.

A counterweight has jammed, leaving a cage forty feet below the landing. The walls contain maintenance ladders, anchor rings and a hand brake. Characters can fly, climb, bridge the gap, repair the brake or move the cage by strength and leverage. Describe these options rather than waiting for a single correct skill.

Run a three-progress work challenge only if the party's method remains uncertain. A setback means lost equipment, noise alerting raiders or a slower approach. A fall uses the campaign's normal falling rules and offers the usual opportunities for rescue; no cinematic automatic death.

Workers describe raiders taking pump tools and water filters. One also reports that Korga's captain spoke of opening the upper gates. This foreshadows genuine conquest alongside the grievance.

**Choice:** Send rescued workers back with soldiers, shelter them in a defensible bay or bring them onward. All are viable with suitable protection.

**Exit:** Steam obscures the contested gallery; the main pressure wheel is turning the wrong way.

## 2.4 - Battle at the Ninth Sluice | 60 minutes

**Actors:** Brakk using the Tunnel Captain profile, two Orc Shock Troopers and two Goblin Sappers. Five allied soldiers hold the entry and are background support, not five additional full turns.

# 9. Session Five - The Living and the Loyal

**Purpose:** Turn private discoveries into public consequences. The party can win this political confrontation. Hadrik is not guaranteed escape, immunity or a later boss appearance.

**Prepared actors:** All three generals, Baldrik, Heddra, Runa, Dagnner, Yrsa, Venn, workers and any Cinder-Tusk witnesses. **Map:** Hammerhall with a public gallery and four ordinary exits. **Milestone:** Level 12 at the end.

**Lower Bridge:** Two Shock Troopers and three Sappers attempt to cut the evacuation path. Keep a marked ten-foot corridor passable for four rounds; allied workers move the civilians. The heroes can reroute the crossing, create a magical bridge or negotiate withdrawal. They need not personally carry every person.

**Pump Galleries:** A Tunnel Captain and three Sappers occupy two controls. Secure both and gain four total work progress before round 5 to prevent damaging flow reversal. Existing water-control magic or the release key can provide an equivalent solution.

Enemy morale breaks when the captain falls or the main objective becomes impossible, unless a specific leader offers credible reinforcements. Victories are determined by outcomes, not corpse counts.

## 7.4 - The shield between them | 25 minutes

**Actors:** Baldrik, Thorgrinn, selected companions and remaining Vanguard soldiers.

During a tactical pause, Baldrik offers his company to protect the party's approach to Korga. He does not demand that Thorgrinn return to the family unit or accept a rank. An accountable Baldrik asks, "Where do you need us?" A defensive Baldrik says, "Stay behind my shield," revealing that his instincts have not fully changed.

The party can accept his help, place him under another command, leave him guarding evacuees or keep him in custody. Each is mechanically viable. A retained competent company removes one ordinary guard from Korga's scene or ensures a retreat route, but does not defeat the boss for the players.

No falling bridge demands his scripted sacrifice. If the players choose a dangerous rear-guard action, resolve it with a stated task and normal chances of escape. Death is a consequence of play, not the price of forgiving him.

**Exit:** Scouts identify Korga at the command bridge directing the next assault.

## 7.5 - Korga's last argument | 55 minutes

**Actors:** Korga and two Shock Troopers, reduced by applicable allies or earlier victories. Urzha can attend under a truce or relay terms.

At a broad bridge overlooking the reservoir, Korga argues that victory is the only guarantee his people will ever have. He admits the campaign now seeks the upper gates. He does not pretend a captured family ration book excuses every order.

There are three resolutions. Combat can kill, capture or drive him from command. A negotiated command challenge can settle his leadership if both sides agreed its terms beforehand; it is not a compulsory duel for Thorgrinn. A credible peace can remove his mandate: A at least 3, a witnessed water agreement, a real end to the purge, and enough military pressure or clan support that continuing costs him his command. Social checks can refine surrender terms, not replace those conditions.

In combat, use Korga's profile and the bridge's marked cover and side stairs. Do not collapse it without warning. At half HP he seeks a better position; at 40 HP with no supporters and a viable surrender offer, he can yield.

**Exit:** The assault ends or loses central coordination. Determine which troops surrender, retreat or remain for an orderly withdrawal.

## 7.6 - Victory, and the pressure gauge | 20 minutes

**Actors:** Runa, Skrik or another surviving engineer; the party.

If Last Bastion remains armed, siege damage and the already documented reversal latch begin driving pressure toward the purge configuration. This is the consequence foreshadowed in Sessions 5-6, not a secret new machine. Hadrik can deliberately accelerate it only if he is actually free and able to reach its controls.

Explain the four-hour horizon, the route and available short rest. The engineers present the same three stations already described. Killing Korga or Hadrik does not repair a pressurized network, but securing or disabling it earlier absolutely prevents this emergency.

If the party already made the system safe, reveal the good news instead. Session 8 opens with inspection, demobilization and settlement, with no fabricated final catastrophe. They have earned a quieter ending.

**Close:** The war horns fall silent. In the unsettled version, a deeper mechanical pulse continues under the stone. In the secure version, the first clean water returns to a lower public fountain.

**Compression and record:** Reduce command discussion before cutting the Korga resolution. Record all front outcomes, Korga's fate, Hadrik's actual status, resources remaining and whether the emergency still exists.

# 12. Session Eight - What We Choose to Keep

**Purpose:** Resolve the physical danger, political responsibility and family question separately. No single boss death repairs every relationship. No character must change body or accept leadership to complete the arc.

**Prepared actors:** The allies and antagonists actually alive, free and present. **Map:** A 90-by-70-foot Heartworks with three regulator platforms, central engine, walkways and two exits. **Milestone:** Level 13 at the conclusion.

## 8.1 - Before the last door | 20 minutes

**Actors:** Thorgrinn, Baldrik if available, the party and engineers.

Allow a short rest inside the established four-hour preparation window, then a final conversation. Baldrik makes no request for instant absolution. If accountable, he tells Thorgrinn he will give evidence and accept judgement. If still evasive, he offers family loyalty in place of accountability; the difference should be obvious without a narrator declaring him redeemed or damned.

Runa offers Thorgrinn a choice of practical repairs or no intervention. She repeats that his body is not the key and his soul is not fuel. Heddra sends a simple message: "There is a place for all of you when this is over."

Players decide who goes into the Heartworks and who supports evacuation. Allies operate background routes, not the player characters' most important actions.

**Secure-system alternative:** Use this scene for a shared inspection that verifies the mountain is safe, followed by preparation for the public settlement.

## 8.2 - The Heartworks opens | 30 minutes

**Actors:** Runa and Skrik as advisers or their recovered notes; Hadrik only if his actual position permits.

Show the three stations before initiative: the reservoir wheel feeds the circuit; the relief channel safely releases pressure; the reversal brake locks the new arrangement. Each needs two progress. At a station, a character can spend an action on a DC 16 appropriate check, make a clearly applicable magical intervention, or use a prepared key for one automatic progress. Once a station has two progress, it stays set unless the party knowingly reverses it.

All three must be completed to stop the system. Tasks can be distributed in any order. A completed relief channel grants one extra round in the coming countdown. A 3-4 Accord engineering team starts one chosen station with one progress; the party must still secure the area and complete the work.

Hadrik, if present, offers to cancel the purge if allowed to retain command. This is leverage over controls, not Thorgrinn. Arrest, negotiation, surrender and immediate attack remain available.

**Secure-system alternative:** Demonstrate the functioning safeguards and authenticate E6 for the public record.

## 8.3 - Last Bastion | 70 minutes

**Actors:** Bastion Engine and, only if still free and present, Hadrik. Use the encounter adjustments in Appendix A; do not add a full-strength second boss merely because both survived.

The engine follows locked military instructions and attacks interference. It contains no captive soul. Explain that disabling or destroying it removes the physical guardian but does not by itself open the relief channel. Conversely, completing all three stations stops the purge and makes the engine stand down: combat is not mandatory to the finish.

The countdown begins at the end of the first combat round after the visible warning bell. Six rounds remain; completing relief adds one. At the end of each round, describe the next marked pressure stage. Successful work is never secretly erased. A full shutdown, a clearly effective major spell or a credible engineered alternative ends the threat.

If time expires, an initial rupture floods a lower industrial bay and closes one approach; it does not instantly kill both populations. The party has two additional rounds to open relief or evacuate through the two marked exits. A later shutdown can still save the hold, with major damage, displacement and a more difficult settlement. Telegraph those consequences before the last choice.

**Secure-system alternative:** Spend the equivalent time on two contested settlement issues chosen by the players, reconstruction decisions and a meaningful memorial scene. Do not insert a replacement villain.

## 8.4 - The record nobody gets to edit | 35 minutes

**Actors:** Brynja, Odran, Urzha, witnesses, the party and defendants who survived.

Hold a provisional public inquiry with independent witnesses. It is not a final trial for every wartime action. Establish immediate protections, custody and a dated future process. Hadrik's orders, Baldrik's report, Korga's attacks and Skrik's sabotage are assessed separately. Saving lives earns gratitude, not blanket immunity.

The party chooses what it advocates: civilian oversight of water, a limited boundary compact, review of military requisition powers, guaranteed hearings for prisoners, protected engineering access and reparations. The society cannot become egalitarian overnight because five adventurers made a speech. A signed, witnessed reform with officials and residents committed to enforcing it is a real victory.

Thorgrinn's death record is corrected independently of whether he takes a commission. Hadrik's attempt to treat his body as military research is rejected unless the party has consciously endorsed such a settlement; even then, ownership is a contested abuse, not narrator-certified truth.

**Exit:** Show who will actually do the work after the party leaves: named clerks, engineers, officers and civilian representatives.

## 8.5 - Brothers, not a verdict | 35 minutes

**Actors:** Thorgrinn, Baldrik if alive, Heddra, Runa and companions as invited.

Return to the family table or the old training yard. The shield is present. Baldrik can offer restitution and honesty, ask for time, accept estrangement or cling to rank. Play the man produced by eight sessions, not the ending you hoped to get.

Thorgrinn may forgive, set conditions, refuse contact, retain affection without trust or ask his brother to help others rather than him. None forfeits the adventure's rewards. A reconciliation should name something concrete: testimony, surrendering command, rebuilding damaged homes or reporting to an independent inquiry. Death does not automatically wash away responsibility; a dead Baldrik leaves testimony, grief and choices about what story to tell.

Ask JP, "What do you want to keep, and what are you not willing to carry for him anymore?" Do not answer for him.

For bodily identity, the default ending is still mechanical. A trustworthy investigation into restoration can begin if Thorgrinn wants it, but the module invents neither his original body's location nor a guaranteed cure. Existing effective player magic is not blocked.

## 8.6 - The next morning | 25 minutes

**Actors:** Every surviving player character, with selected NPCs in short epilogue beats.

Give each player a final choice: a responsibility accepted, a relationship changed, a project funded, a possession left behind or a road they intend to take. Damian may negotiate a lasting supply agreement; Vesper may establish protected care; Legna may revise an oath; Bunch may help create a communal signal system. These are examples, not assigned endings.

Reward the group with an actual safe base, trusted craftspeople, reciprocal travel rights where negotiated, and payment or material support appropriate to their already substantial resources. Do not drown an already powerful party in another set of oversized weapons.

Optional family reward: the two-rivet shield becomes a consensual symbol, not compulsory property transferred from Baldrik. The mechanical reward in Appendix A can instead be placed on a new badge shared with the entire party.

Final image: clean water entering a lower cistern, a gate held open rather than shut, or two brothers standing at the same wall with room between them. Choose the image that matches their decisions.

**Compression and record:** Never sacrifice the final family conversation to extend a boss fight. Remove remaining ordinary enemies when their objectives collapse. Complete the milestone and close the eight-session arc here, without a compulsory ninth-session villain reveal.

# Appendix A. Encounter profiles and table procedures

## Scope and shared rules

These compact profiles are original homebrew scaffolding for this party, not official monster statistics or a claim of tested balance. They supply the numbers needed for the planned encounters. Use existing superior NPC sheets where available. Ability modifiers below run in the order Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma. Unlisted saves use the relevant ability modifier; unlisted skills do likewise. All ordinary combatants speak the languages reasonably established for them, can perceive their immediate battlefield and use normal movement, reactions, cover, grappling and concentration under your table's rules. Their ancestry does not give them unexplained immunity to player abilities.

All listed attacks use the stated attack bonus and deal the dice damage on a hit. A creature has one reaction each round. Legendary actions refresh at the start of its turn and occur at the end of another creature's turn, one option at a time. Recharge 5-6 means roll a d6 at the start of the creature's turn and regain the feature on 5 or 6. Use the party's established critical-hit rule. Morale guidelines are starting dispositions, not magical compulsion.

The strongest encounters assume five well-equipped PCs near the planned level. For four PCs, remove one supporting enemy and reduce a boss's HP by about 15 percent before initiative. For six PCs, add one ordinary support enemy or an exposed secondary objective rather than simply raising every AC. If the party's unusual equipment makes a fight easy, let it be easy; preserve strategic stakes through the stated objectives, not secret mid-fight HP inflation.

# Appendix C. Branches, endings and continuity protection

## Branches that must stay real

**Hadrik is exposed or killed early:** The political conspiracy loses its coordinator. Give the party the benefit: better legal access, weakened loyalists and easier testimony. Korga's independent offensive still exists. The armed engineering system can be disarmed early too. Never invent a hidden superior who repeats Hadrik's exact plot.

**Baldrik is rejected:** Keep his grief and decisions independent of Thorgrinn's affection. He can act honourably without being rewarded with reunion. Alternatively, he can remain defensive and face the consequences. The party is not responsible for preventing every bad decision he makes.

**Baldrik dies:** Record who killed him and why. His documents, witnesses and the surviving family carry the inquiry forward. His death does not force Thorgrinn to mourn in a prescribed way or erase responsibility.

**The party supports the council:** Military service remains playable. It does not require approving collective punishment. They can defeat Korga, protect civilians and retain a stricter political order while still exposing the purge. If they knowingly support abusive policies, show the actual effects without secretly granting the reform ending.

**The party supports the Cinder-Tusk:** Urzha still requires accountability for her own side, and Korga still threatens the upper hold. The party can protect the clan without crowning its war-chief or abandoning dwarven civilians.

**Magic reveals the conspiracy immediately:** Shift scenes from discovering truth to making it actionable: authentication, protecting witnesses, organizing a viable alternative and preventing the offensive. Do not make every villain inexplicably immune to truth magic.

**Thorgrinn seeks restoration:** Support his chosen inquiry while preserving his autonomy. Runa can repair a chassis and Yrsa can advise; neither is suddenly able to recreate his original flesh. A valid existing spell or known specialist may change the outcome if the actual campaign rules permit. Restoration is not the admission price to family or society.

**The party refuses all involvement:** Let them leave. The planned arc then does not occur as written. Offer consequences and opportunities, not a curse or confiscation that traps them. An eight-session plan cannot honestly guarantee eight sessions regardless of every possible player choice.

## Secure-system Session 8: substantive play without a fake boss

When the party prevents Last Bastion before the siege ends, use the saved 70-minute encounter slot for two issues selected by the group.

**Issue A - Who controls the water?** Odran wants one accountable authority; Skrik refuses another unilateral diversion; Runa proposes paired oversight and published measurements. The party chooses oversight, emergency powers and an appeal process. Establish who can stop flow, who witnesses the action, and how a threatened community can object. Resolve remaining uncertainty with targeted checks about trust or logistics, not whether the party guesses the designer's preferred constitution.

**Issue B - What does justice require?** Victims want named responsibility, soldiers fear collective prosecution and Baldrik's family fears a convenient scapegoat. The party decides immediate custody, witness protection, restitution and who hears the inquiry. They cannot personally guarantee perfect justice, but they can create a credible process and expose evasions.

**Issue C - What gets rebuilt first?** Water, homes, military stores and access roads compete for finite labour and funds. The party's treasure can genuinely expand capacity. Let each player back a project and identify its beneficiary. Heddra and the workers can disagree with the generals without being secret villains.

**Issue D - Which dead are named?** A joint memorial raises disputes over combatants, executed deserters and civilians. The scene concerns honest remembrance, not forced forgiveness. Thorgrinn may include people the old society erased or refuse to let a general describe the dead as an acceptable expense.

## Ending combinations

**Home with boundaries:** The hold is safe; Thorgrinn is recognized without enlistment; Baldrik accepts responsibility; the brothers retain contact on terms JP chooses. Their relationship is beginning repair, not magically cured.

**The honoured dissenter:** The party wins the war and prevents the purge but secures only limited reform. Thorgrinn rejects a commission and retains allies among workers, engineers and soldiers. The home is worth loving without being worth obeying blindly.

**A new compact:** Strong Accord and real implementation produce shared water governance, reliable prisoner protections and monitored military powers. Urzha and Brynja remain wary partners; an orc and goblin clan has not become a dwarven client people.

**Victory with estrangement:** Baldrik refuses accountability or commits further abuses. The party still saves the mountain. Thorgrinn's refusal to reconcile is an ending, not a failure state.

**The costly rescue:** The purge partially activates or defence fails. The hold survives with displaced people, damaged districts and an urgent reconstruction obligation. Do not narrate a clean triumph; do not erase the lives the party did save.

**The military solution:** Korga is defeated and the council retains most of its structure. The party may have stopped the worst abuse without changing the caste system. Show both security gained and injustice left unresolved. Do not present a new king as the natural reward in a society established as a stratocracy.

## Preparation and session-end ledger

Before each session, select the actual surviving cast; mark Hadrik's and Baldrik's locations; place the essential clues at at least two accessible sources; review player promises; and choose only the relevant encounter variant. Prepare one principal tactical map, not every possible branch.

After each session, record D, A, evidence obtained and copied, prisoner names and custody, public promises, actual civilian damage, major NPC loyalties and the three Baldrik accountability events. Also record what the PCs told the public, so propaganda disputes refer to things that really happened.

Before Sessions 7 and 8, review current HP, spell resources, summons, companions, unusually strong items and player-built solutions. Set encounter adjustments openly through fiction before initiative. Do not quietly neutralize the abilities the players earned in Barovia.
